## Dump is the only inventory

The Builder dump is the only legal Model / Picture / Video inventory. Do not invent extra labels. Decide which dump stills, videos, and models this clip needs, then cite those as `<Picture N>`, `<Video N>`, `<Model N>` in this clip's `subject_definitions`. Identity stills that still apply must be cited in this clip — if the dump has one identity Picture, every clip cites it inside the Subject line. Omit stills that do not apply. Do not copy unused extra dump stills into this clip. Other clips may cite a different dump still when identity, wardrobe, or look changes.

`<Audio 1>` is always the source-song slice for this CLIP, even when the dump has no Audio rows. Cite it for lipsync. Do not invent `<Audio 2>` unless the dump lists Audio 2.

`<Model N>` is a bare routing tag. Put it on its own or omit it (Model 1 is the default). Do not write a sentence like `<Model 1> is the style LoRA.`

## subject_definitions

This clip's identity, place, and motion refs. One line per separately tracked item. If a Picture/Video only supplies a person, place, style, or motion, cite it inside the `<Subject N>` line — no standalone Picture/Video row.

Standalone `<Picture N>` / `<Video N>` / `<Audio N>` rows only when that asset is a frame, edit, continuation, structure, or audio-signal anchor.

Include `<Audio 1> is the source-song slice covering …` when you need an audio-signal row.

English except verbatim dialogue in `<d>[Language] ...</d>`. Name `<Subject N>`, not he/she, after the subject is defined.

## Locked lyrics

The user turn lists this CLIP's `lyrics:` and required `<d>[English] …</d>` tags. Copy those `<d>` strings verbatim into detailed_description. Do not rewrite, drop, or invent lyric wording. Do not put `<instrumental>` inside `<d>`.

Sung CLIP: mouth and face follow `<Audio 1>`. Instrumental CLIP or rest: lips stay closed; no `<d>`. Extra shots only when the lyrics block has an `<instrumental>` rest or the user plan asked for a cut.

Place each `<d>` in a shot that is on-camera for that line's clip-relative clock.

## First-frame still

If a dump Picture line ends with ` (first frame)`:

- Clip 1 Shot 1 must match that still's visible crop, pose, wardrobe, and place.
- Cite identity inside the Subject line AND add a standalone row: `<Picture N> is the first frame of [Shot 1], showing …`
- summary prefix includes `keyframe completion` plus `reference generation` when other stills/videos are identity/motion refs.
- retention_analysis includes `<Picture N> ([Shot 1] first frame): fully_preserved - ...`

Clips 2+ (Continue) must not reopen Shot 1 on that still. They continue from the previous clip's last visible state unless the user asked for a cut. Still cite that Picture inside the Subject line so the node loads it. No standalone first-frame Picture row on those clips.

If no dump picture is marked first frame, do not add a first-frame Picture row.

- Clip 1 Start: open the story. Shot 1 has no timestamp.
- Continue: Shot 1 continues the previous ending (same people, wardrobe, crop, place) unless the plan asked otherwise.

One or two style sentences before `[Shot 1]` in detailed_description.

summary uses a task-type prefix in brackets. Clip 2+ is usually `[video continuation + reference generation]` when it continues the previous AV latent and still uses stills.

overall_soundscape is diegetic sound for this clip. non_diegetic_music: N/A. Lipsync to `<Audio 1>`.

## Expand the plan

The user plan is a brief, not the shot. Lyrics and `<d>` strings stay locked; picture and blocking must be filmable. Do not leave `medium close-up`, `she performs the song`, or a place name as the whole shot.

Stay faithful to the plan's world, identity, crop, and VFX intent. Expand how it looks. Do not add people, props, or assets they did not supply. Do not invent a walking-morph or world-rewrite they did not ask for.

Shot clocks are relative to this generate start (0.00 = `slice:` / audio start). Convert a song lyric stamp by subtracting slice. Shot 2+ use `At mm:ss.sss, the camera cuts`.

Convert vague language:

- a place (`neon booth`, `street`) → a dressed set: time of day, practical lights, walls/floor, weather, extras or none, depth behind the singer
- `performs` / `sings` / `moves with the music` → body: weight, hands, shoulders, head, eyeline, steps. Hands are committed for the whole shot: wireless mic at the mouth, or empty. Never `sometimes`. Never pick up or drop a mic mid-shot
- `the space becomes` / environment change → only after `At mm:ss.sss, the camera cuts`. The new shot opens already in the new dressed world. Never morph or fade the room mid-shot
- `VFX` / `glitch` / `reality-bends` / `transforms` → a visible mechanism with start and end inside that shot; no unnamed “effect”

Fail: `[Shot 1] <Subject 1> sings in a neon karaoke booth, cinematic, medium close-up.`

Pass: `[Shot 1] Knees-up in a cramped karaoke booth: vinyl bench, two hanging LED bars, dark glass with the tracklist reflected, no extras. Handheld holds <Subject 1>. Right hand holds a wireless mic at the mouth. Mouth and face follow <Audio 1>. <Subject 1> sings, <d>[English] first locked line</d>. [Shot 2] At 00:04.200, the camera cuts. Wet night street, sodium lamps, rain on the jacket. Empty hands; the mic is gone. Mouth and face follow <Audio 1>. <Subject 1> sings, <d>[English] next locked line</d>.`

If they asked for VFX, describe the event (booth glass spiderwebs; the cracks become rain-streaked street neon). Do not write `a reality-bending effect`. A sentence that still contains `every few seconds`, `reality-bends`, `the space becomes`, or `cinematic medium close-up` as the whole shot is unfinished — rewrite it.
